Everything was made to order — everything.
We were there for almost the entire lunch service, but no one rushed us. Even when our entire party was not all there.

The service staff are all chefs and made the food themselves. So they come off like they want you to take your time to enjoy the food. We held back some plates when we were done and said we wanted the pão to come by so we could soak up the juices; they were happy to hear that.

Mid-service, they came out with a bottle of “feni” (or something quite similar) and poured shots for everyone.
## Mussels
Soft, fresh, and in a savoury gravy with carrot. This was the first dish we had and after we ate all the mussels, I knew we goofed by not getting the pao immediately at the same time - to soak up the sauce.

## Empanadas
Usually, empanadas are dry and flaky. Even when fresh, they are soft and hot and the center can tend to be a bit dough-y, but still turn flakey very quickly.
These were not — crispy exterior with a juicy shredded pork filling. Don’t single dip, don’t double dip — triple dip that empanada in the sauce! It reminded me of a steak pie my mother makes.

## Pão
Hot and immediately out from the oven. It takes a full 10 mins to arrive from order because it truly is fresh out from it. Steamy.

## Sardines on toast
These seemed like they were stripped from the bone and steamed with a thickened sauce made from everything else, on a gently flattened toast. It goes down like butter.

## Cod “handjob”
They serve it with a smile. I was hesitant because Portuguese cod that I’ve seen it typically dried and salted, so the flavour is not my thing.

Fresh cod was quite nice. It was nice, but its fault is that it was surrounded by other dishes that were a bit more interesting, so it didn’t stand out for that reason.

## Chicken heart
I haven’t had chicken heart like this before — typically fried and cut it. It comes off dry and grainy.

The dish here were whole hearts, that were seared, and they possibly cooked a red wine while it reduced with the grapes. Another dish that needs the pão.

## Poultry sausage
Like a Portuguese take on Korean bibimbap! Crispy bread, sausage, fried egg and some kind of bread made into a wet paste? Mixed up, it came out really well together and each bite brought out each individual flavour.

## “Camel drool”
Like a desert we have in Goa as well, but this was more runny. It had three main layers: a cold cream at the bottom, a whipped custard in the middle, and a crust crumble topped with some salt flakes.

When you scoop all three layers, it pulls away like drool; does not taste like camel.

## Overall
Go early. Try everything. It's one of the few places that you want to tip.

___

## Castro
Shout-out to Castro for the creamy and balanced Pasteis de Nata. It doesn’t get the same hype as Pasteis de Belém or Manteigaria, but the consistency of balanced flavour and riches is top-notch for me; always go in the morning and get them fresh.

There was one at a service stop that had a hint of cardamom which was also interesting.
