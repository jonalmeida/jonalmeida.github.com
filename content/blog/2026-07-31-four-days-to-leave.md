In April, I had to attend a conference in Paris and I convinced my wife to attend so that could make a short holiday out of it at the end of the week before we head back home.

We planned to spend four days there, but it ended up turning into eight because of airline delays, with those four extra days at the airport and the airport hotels.

Hilariously, the flight out was also entertaining, so it's going to be part 1 of the whole trip because I can't write as much as this needs from me all in one sitting.

## The outbound flight

If this was a movie, it would have been considered foreshadowing. We arrive at the airport early, fresh, and comfortable. We have enough time to roam around the restaurants to look for some snacks for the flight, or maybe a light meal. 

Eventually, we sit at our gate and while away our time. I pull out my laptop and get to some work - code reviews or cleaning up some patches to put for one, I don't remember. We hear that the flight is 30 minutes delayed because the inbound flight hasn't arrived yet. No worries, I'll continue my work for now. 

The plane lands. Well, due to the delay the plane needs to be clean and cleared before boarding can behind - fine.

Alright, boarding begins and everyone has queued in the front despite the airline staff asking people to not do that, while they board by individual zones. We stay where we are in our seats and wait for our zone to be called, and eventually we get there on to the plane.

People start to settle in. We see the flight attendants do their final checks, and then captain starts with some announcements: "Folks, I spoke with one of the passengers, who said she would really like to make it in time to see her daughter [for her birthday?], and rest assured we can make up the time we've lost in the air." - nice!

We're get comfortable as the plane leaves the gate and moves into a conga line of planes that are also heading to the runway. We kept moving and stopping as we made our way in it. We end up stopping again but it didn't strike me as odd, until some flight attendants were moving to the back of the plane. Sure, I guess, some last minute logistics.

A new announcement, "Sorry for the delay, we have some passengers with a sick child who have opted to deboard the flight. We're going to make our way to the gate."

Totally reasonable - we don't know what the situation was, but it was reasonable to decide to do want to do so. Thankfully, we find out later that it wasn't life threatening or requiring emergency services. However, we're in that conga line, remember? We can't dip out from it at any point. So we followed it through some more and eventually get out - now we get to a gate. This took about an hour.

"Alright, the parents and their kid have gotten off the plane. We're going to have to get their luggage out too. Please bear with us, it won't be too long." 

It was another 30 minutes.

"We just wanted to update you that the baggage was removed. However, with the delays we're now crossed over past midnight. Canadian aviation regulation states that if you're flying over the Atlantic at night, you need to fly with three pilots and we've got only two. Don't worry though, we've called in our standby pilot and they are heading from their home in Etobicoke, so it should only be about 20 minutes."

The airline crew come around to hand out water and snacks to some folks. We're surprised, but also in good spirits - we snacked before we got on, we have nice seats (by the aisle for me) and people around us were quite pleasant. No one was getting frustrated or shouty - that was nice. We had some podcasts downloaded, so we listened to that and it was more than 20 minutes later before the next announcement, that's for sure.

"Alright, our third pilot has arrived and we should be ready to go soon. However, since we've been waiting, we've burnt away some fuel, so we're going to have to refuel before we head out. It shouldn't be much longer, thanks for your patience."

You honestly can't help but laugh at this point. It was a sitcom episode and the laugh-track was playing in our heads: Lemony Snicket's A Series of Comical Events. 

We giggle amongst ourselves because we thought it was hilarious. We're still in a good mood because we have each other and having company through this makes it easier to enjoy the experience.
